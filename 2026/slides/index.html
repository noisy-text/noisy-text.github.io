Keynote 1

When Noise Is the Point: What Humanistic Tasks Reveal About Our Data Pipelines

Maria Antoniak · University of Colorado Boulder

Chair: Alan Ramponi

Introduction (from the speaker's bio): Maria Antoniak is an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at the University of Colorado Boulder, affiliated with the Department of Information Science and the Boulder NLP Group. Previously she was a Young Investigator at the Allen Institute for AI and a postdoc at the Pioneer Centre for AI, University of Copenhagen. She completed her PhD in Information Science at Cornell and her master's in Computational Linguistics at the University of Washington. Her research is in NLP and cultural analytics. Talk: three settings where humanistic data frustrates standard pipelines (creative requests in WildChat, narrative annotation of web pretraining data, and poetry formatting lost in linearization), and why what counts as noise depends on who is reading.

Keynote 2

Language Identification in the (Pretraining) Data Processing Pipeline – State of the Art and Open Problems

Hinrich Schütze · University of Munich

Chair: JinYeong Bak

Introduction (from the speaker's bio): Hinrich Schütze is Professor for Computational Linguistics and co-director of the Center for Information and Language Processing at LMU Munich. He received his PhD from Stanford in 1995 for early research on embeddings, which won the ACL Test-of-Time Paper Award. He worked at Xerox PARC, several Silicon Valley startups and Google. He co-authored Foundations of Statistical Natural Language Processing and Introduction to Information Retrieval, received an ERC Advanced Grant in 2017, and was ACL president in 2020. He is a member of Academia Europaea and a fellow of ACL, ELLIS, ELIZA and HessianAI. Talk: language identification (LID) as a step in building multilingual pretraining data, the challenges of thousands of hard-to-distinguish classes and the mismatch between LID training data and pretraining data, why LID measures may not track LM quality, and GlotLID with its design decisions (noise-category labels, composite ISO-639 + script labels, fewer confusable labels, speed vs accuracy).

Keynote 3

Enhancing Multilingual LLMs with Code-Switching

Haneul Yoo · New York University

Chair: Weerayut Buaphet

Introduction (from the speaker's bio): Haneul Yoo is an Assistant Professor/Faculty Fellow in the Department of Computer Science, Courant Institute, New York University, and a Research Fellow with the United Nations Department of Peace Operations. She received her PhD from KAIST. Her work covers language models that generalize across languages, resources and evaluation for underrepresented languages and cultures, and AI for social good. She has published at ACL, EMNLP and NAACL, was named a Rising Star at KAIST exploreCSR, and has co-organized the MELT workshop at COLM 2025, the WiML symposium at ICML 2026, and GenAI4World at COLM 2026. Talk: reframes code-switching as a linguistic signal across evaluation, training and inference: output language alignment (OLA), code-switching red-teaming (CSRT), curriculum learning (CSCL) and in-context learning (CSICL).
